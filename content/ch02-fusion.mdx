<Margin tag="§ 02 · fusion">
The summer after my sophomore year, at a private fusion startup. I showed up
as a computational physicist and left knowing what it means to *own* a
parameter scan.
</Margin>

<Pen>
The reactor is always smarter than the simulation of the reactor.
</Pen>

<Body>

A fusion reactor has more knobs than a recording studio — a simulation of a fusion reactor more still.
My job for the summer was to map that parameter space: to push a simulation
of the reactor through thousands of configurations and ask, at each point,
*what is the plasma doing physically, why, and how does that inform the next
set of values we want to simulate?*

The clever-sounding part is the search. The useful part is building enough
physical intuition that while the multi-day simulations are running, you can look at a new
regime and already know which plot to make. You are not really exploring a
parameter space. You are building a prior over one. I'll readily admit that I
simply borrowed this intuition from plasma physicists far smarter than myself.
Still, I internalized a few things along the way.

The lesson: if you design the *measurement* right — in this case, the right
reduced observables on the right 2-D slice, visualized in the beautiful
physics tool Paraxial — the physics comes out the other end practically for
free. The skill isn't math. The skill is taste in what to measure.
